Why does cycle length matter?

A 28-day cycle is the conventional reference. Differences in cycle length can change the estimated ovulation timing, so this calculator adjusts the LMP estimate by the difference between your average cycle length and 28 days. This remains an estimate and does not replace clinical dating.

Pregnancy Calculator Guide

Use the sections below to understand how each dating method works, what the results mean, and where the calculator's estimates have limits.

How does this pregnancy calculator work?

This calculator supports several common pregnancy-dating inputs rather than forcing every pregnancy into one formula. The result is built around an estimated due date, then the calculator works backward or forward from that date to show gestational age, trimester, pregnancy progress and key week-based dates.

Pregnancy due date calculation formulas
Core dating formulas
LMP: EDD = LMP + 280 days + (average cycle length − 28 days)
Conception: EDD = conception date + 266 days
IVF/ART: EDD = transfer date + (266 − embryo age in days)
Ultrasound dating: EDD = scan date + (280 − gestational age at scan)

The conventional pregnancy dating framework counts 280 days, or 40 weeks, from the first day of the last menstrual period. Conception-based dating uses approximately 266 days from conception.

Clinical references: ACOG: Methods for Estimating the Due Date and NHS: 12-week scan.

How many weeks pregnant am I?

Gestational age is normally expressed as completed weeks plus days. For example, 8 weeks and 3 days is written as 8 weeks 3 days. This calculator uses the selected dating anchor to estimate elapsed gestational days. If the entered dates place the estimated start in the future, check the inputs and confirm dating with your healthcare professional.

Pregnancy trimesters and term categories

First trimester: through 13 weeks 6 days. Second trimester: 14 weeks 0 days through 27 weeks 6 days. Third trimester: from 28 weeks 0 days onward. Separately, ACOG classifies 37+0–38+6 as early term, 39+0–40+6 as full term, 41+0–41+6 as late term, and 42+0 or later as postterm.

Ultrasound pregnancy dating

If your ultrasound report already gives a gestational age, you can enter the scan date and that reported gestational age. First-trimester ultrasound is the most accurate method to establish or confirm gestational age according to ACOG. The calculator does not interpret ultrasound images or calculate gestational age from fetal measurements.

IVF and assisted reproductive technology dating

For pregnancies resulting from assisted reproductive technology, embryo transfer date and embryo age provide a specific dating anchor. ACOG explicitly gives a Day-3 offset of 263 days and a Day-5 offset of 261 days from transfer; Day 6 uses 260 days based on equivalent embryo development days; confirm the official date with your fertility team.
